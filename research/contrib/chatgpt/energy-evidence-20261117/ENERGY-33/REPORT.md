# ENERGY-33 — One-change-point energy versus matched Bayesian causal filtering

**Date:** 2026-11-18. **Recorded decision:** `DEVELOPMENT_NO_GO`. **Native Yggdrasil training:** NOT RUN. **Scientific acceptance:** NOT CLAIMED. **Deployment:** NONE.

## Causal question
Can latent, non-oracle single-change-point inference preserve existing causal knowledge under observation noise while adapting to novel/familiar changed rules more effectively than complete conventional Bayesian alternatives? An exact energy formulation and an independently coded conventional Bayesian factor-product reference are included to test their mathematical equivalence. This is a **source-free synthetic qualifier**, not a new architecture or open-ended causal concept induction.

## Primary results

| Scenario (80 worlds / 960 held actions) | Energy CP | Bayesian CP | Fixed hazard | Recent 16 | Full history |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Stationary familiar law | 950 | 950 | 951 | 851 | **960** |
| Novel composite causal law | 626 | 626 | 623 | **661** | 525 |
| Switch to another familiar law | 787 | 787 | 786 | **860** | 578 |

The strongest fair control is recent-window Bayes on both change cohorts; the new energy mechanism loses by **35/960 = 3.65 percentage points** on novel changes and **73/960 = 7.60 points** on familiar switches. Stationary energy accuracy is 950/960 (98.96%), versus full-history 960/960 (100%), missing the frozen within-one-percentage-point requirement by a fraction of one point. **False stationary change alarms: 7/80 = 8.75%**, under the prespecified 10% cap. This is a different seeded population and mechanism from ENERGY-32's 15/80 false alarms: the rates must **not** be described as a controlled paired improvement.

Frozen development gates: **false-alarm limit PASS; energy/Bayes parity PASS; both change-accuracy gates FAIL; stationary non-regression gate FAIL; energy-specific distinctiveness FAIL by mathematical equivalence.** Overall `DEVELOPMENT_NO_GO`. We did not tune the threshold, priors, seeds, split or outcomes after primary scoring.
