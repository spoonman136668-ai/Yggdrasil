# FKP18-CB3 — Discover which public features encode relationships

**Result: PUBLIC DEVELOPMENT MIXED / NO-GO for semantic gate-role recovery and unique learning advantage. Research-only, no native integration.**

## Why this experiment exists

FKP18-CB2 successfully kept four context-conditioned relationships separate during delayed-feedback interference and repaired a changed relationship without overwriting three retained ones, but the four mode labels were supplied from `R7&3`. CB3 removes that supplied gate: candidate and equally informed conventional control both see **the same 38 generic binary features, the same 512 earned delayed binary-action labels per world, and no private source-law/gate indices**.

The candidate first discovers a strong two-feature relation (703 pairs), then uses its residual contradictions to search pairs of other public features for a four-way conditional decomposition and a learned sixteen-bin truth table. The independent conventional comparator searches **all C(38,4)=73,815 unordered four-feature sets**, fitting an unconstrained 16-bit truth table under the same public data. This conventional comparator is at least as expressive as the candidate and is a necessary scientific control. Both are source-blind and no-gradient.

## Frozen results: learning sufficient features is not knowing their causal role

A public world has eight uint4 registers, two legal binary actions within FKP17's eight-way action schema, and delayed +1/-1 reward at time `t` for the actual action taken at `t−2`. The evaluator's hidden law depends on four generic observable bit features: two are **source-defined relation content** and two are **source-defined gate features**. The source evaluator keeps the indices/formula separate from both models; they choose an action before being scored on its source-defined consequence. These are synthetic public one-action score queries, **not** an independent multi-step closed-loop challenge.

Each of sixteen synthetic public worlds used 515 fixed shared training actions (512 delayed credits), and each of six arms was scored on 128 newly sampled public contexts, without receiving feedback from scored contexts. Total 8,240 real synthetic training actions, 8,192 earned credits, and 12,288 read-only action/consequence scoring queries.

| Independent exhaustive conventional four-feature rule | **100.00%** (2,048/2,048) |

**Actual four relevant feature ID sets: 16/16 worlds recovered.** All sixteen learned 16-bin decision tables fit 512/512 earned public examples and generalize 128/128 on new contexts. The candidate's cold 78-byte versioned/hash-protected checkpoint exactly restores every scored choice, and rejects corrupted snapshots.

**But** the exact source-private `CONTENT` pair and `GATE` pair are each recovered in only **5/16** worlds, not the preregistered **at least 12/16**. Candidate minus independent exhaustive symbolic **0.0**, 1,000-world-resampling 95% CI **[0,0]**; both frozen strong-control superiority gates also FAIL. This is **not** a scientific, native-acceptance or full engineering PASS.

### What the semantic failure teaches

The latent role target is **not identifiable** in this experiment's hypothesis family: a general 16-entry Boolean truth table over four input bits has exactly the same expressive power after permuting the positions called 'gate' and 'content', with a corresponding permutation of its table indexing. Every scored input/output relation remains identical. Without extra structural constraints or interventions specifically identifying gate invariance versus content variation, passive delayed action/reward examples cannot establish the source author's intended naming.

This is a *post-result structural diagnosis*, not a replacement outcome metric and not a claim that the 12/16 gate-role gate actually passed. It explains why a model can learn perfectly predictive relationships while failing to infer the supposedly privileged causal decomposition. Giving it more of the same observations cannot resolve a symmetry that is genuinely observationally equivalent.
