## Question and competing explanations

NLD-01 showed the native Yggdrasil local learner fits known disclosed A and B source-free public laws and retains A **with three separate public family-specific banks**; conventional fully fitted comparators matched it. This NLD-02 removes both the three-bank representation and the family label from the learner to isolate **representation and task-identifiability versus memory retention**.

- **H1 (retention):** A single 10-cell shared local predictive state updated from public action/delayed success improves on A, changes legal decisions versus erased state, and *might* retain A competence after unrelated public B learning.
- **H0 (interference/identifiability):** Training B overwrites A under the single-state representation; the gain is matched or exceeded by an equally informed trained shared perceptron and shared episodic action-memory alternative; or no learner can choose accurately for both simultaneous source laws without task/context input.
- **Critical information caveat (preregistered, not discovered afterward):** The original disclosed A rule is `sensor0 XOR sensor1`; B is `sensor2 XOR sensor3`. They disagree for **8 of 16** possible identical public four-bit sensor patterns. When public task/family identity is deliberately hidden from the learner and no prior-task history is provided at scoring, a single deterministic prediction for the same sensors **cannot be correct on both A and B** on the conflicting patterns. With uniform balanced joint A/B scoring, its best attainable mean is **75%**, even with ideal retention. Thus *failure to jointly score 100% is not by itself a consolidation failure*; it is an observable-information limit. Under this interface retention is assessed descriptively on the A-after-B evaluations, and a conflict certificate must accompany any negative.
